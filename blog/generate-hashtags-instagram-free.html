How to Generate Hashtags for Instagram Free

📅 July 2026  ·  ✍️ NexKitTool Editorial Team  ·  ⏱️ 10 min read

You spend an hour taking the perfect photo, another twenty minutes tweaking the contrast, and finally, you are ready to post. But then you stare at a blank caption box, wondering what hashtags to use so your masterpiece actually gets seen. We have all been there, just typing whatever comes to mind and hoping the algorithm notices. Picking the right tags can feel like throwing darts blindfolded. You know they matter, but figuring out which ones actually bring in likes and new followers is exhausting. What if I told you there is a way to stop guessing? You can completely automate this part of your routine. By using a free hashtag generator, you take the guesswork out of the equation and give your posts the reach they deserve. Let me walk you through how you can easily find the perfect tags for your content without spending a single dime.

When it comes to writing with AI, getting a good mix of automated drafting and personal editing is key. This guide walks you through how to use writing assistants effectively, sharing tips to polish your drafts, adjust tones, and make your final text shine.

Why you still need hashtags right now

You might have heard rumors that hashtags are dead. That is simply not true. While Instagram’s algorithm has evolved to read context and visual clues, hashtags still act as a crucial signpost. Think of them as tiny, highly specific search engines within the app. When someone is looking for a specific type of content, like home organization tips or vintage watch collecting, they search for those exact tags. If your post does not include them, you are essentially making your content invisible to people who are actively looking for it. Plus, hashtags help categorize your account over time. When you consistently use relevant tags, you teach the platform what your page is all about. This makes it easier for the algorithm to recommend your content to similar users on the Explore page. Using a random mix of hugely popular tags won't cut it anymore. You need a targeted strategy, and that is exactly where a smart generator comes in.

How to stop picking the wrong tags

One of the biggest mistakes people make is using tags that are way too big. If you throw a tag like 'love' or 'photography' on your post, it will be buried under millions of other pictures in literally seconds. Your content stands zero chance of being seen by anyone other than bots. You need a mix of broad and highly specific tags. This is often called the 'ladder strategy.' You want some tags that have a few million posts, some with a few hundred thousand, and a bunch of very niche tags with under fifty thousand posts. Manually finding this perfect mix takes forever. You have to type things into the search bar, check the post count, write it down, and repeat. A generator does all this heavy lifting for you. It analyzes your main keyword and spits out a balanced mix of tags that you can actually rank for. You just copy, paste, and watch the engagement roll in.

Steps to create your perfect hashtag list

Ready to make your life easier? Follow these quick steps to get a customized list of hashtags for your next post. 1. Open up a free Instagram hashtag generator on your phone or computer. 2. Type in one or two main keywords that describe your photo perfectly. Be specific. If it is a picture of your dog at the beach, type 'golden retriever beach' rather than just 'dog'. 3. Hit the generate button and wait a few seconds for the tool to do its magic. 4. Review the list it gives you. Remove any tags that don't quite fit your specific image or brand voice. 5. Copy the finalized list and paste it into your Instagram caption or the first comment right after you publish.

Where should you actually put them?

This is a debate that has raged on for years: caption or first comment? Honestly, Instagram has officially stated that putting them in the caption is slightly better for searchability. However, many creators still prefer the first comment because it keeps the caption looking clean and easy to read. My advice? Try both and see what your audience prefers. If you do put them in the caption, leave some space between your main text and the tags. You can use periods or special characters on separate lines to push the tags down so they don't distract from your carefully crafted message. The most important thing is that you actually include them. Whether they sit at the bottom of your caption or in the first comment, they are still doing the work of categorizing your post and helping new people find your page.

Keeping your hashtag sets fresh

Another trap people fall into is copying and pasting the exact same block of thirty hashtags on every single post. Instagram hates this. The platform might flag your account for spam behavior if you do this too often. You need to keep things fresh. Create a few different 'sets' of tags related to the main themes you post about. If you are a food blogger, you might have one set for recipes, one for restaurant reviews, and one for behind-the-scenes kitchen prep. Rotate these sets so your posts always look organic to the algorithm. A generator is great for this because you can quickly run a few different keywords and build up these distinct lists in just a few minutes. Save them in the notes app on your phone, and you will always be prepared when it is time to publish.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many hashtags should I use on Instagram?

You can use up to 30 hashtags per post. Some people swear by using exactly 30, while others prefer around 10 to 15 highly targeted ones. Experiment to see what brings you the best results.

Are banned hashtags a real thing?

Yes, they absolutely are. Instagram sometimes bans tags that get overrun with spam or inappropriate content. If you use a banned tag, your post might be hidden. A good generator will usually filter these out for you.

Do hashtags work on Instagram Reels?

They definitely do. Just like normal posts, hashtags help categorize your Reels so they show up for people interested in that specific topic.

Can I edit a post to add hashtags later?

You can, but it is much less effective. The algorithm pays the most attention to a post in the first few hours after it goes live. Adding tags days later won't give you the same boost.

Why am I not getting likes even with hashtags?

Hashtags just get your post in front of people. If the photo isn't engaging or the caption is boring, people still won't engage. Make sure your content is high quality first.
