Why Convert PDF Pages to JPG Images?

Converting PDF documents to image formats like JPG offers several advantages for web workflows and presentations:
• Easy Sharing: JPG files can be uploaded directly to social media platforms, messaging apps, and image galleries.
• Visual Presentation: Insert specific PDF pages as images into PowerPoint slides or Google Docs without layout issues.
• Faster Previews: JPG files load quickly in browsers without requiring PDF readers or plugins. This is ideal for showcasing portfolio pages.

DPI & Resolution Settings Explained

The quality of your output JPG is determined by the DPI (Dots Per Inch) setting used during rendering. Choosing the right density is vital:
• 72 DPI (Web Resolution): Ideal for quick previews or small file sizes. Perfect for mobile screens, email attachments, and chats.
• 150 DPI (Standard Quality): Great for presentations, web uploads, and general screen viewing. Offers a good balance of size and clarity.
• 300 DPI (High Resolution): Necessary for professional printing, marketing assets, and detailed diagrams where text clarity is key. This is the optimal setting.

Extracting Images vs. Converting Entire Pages

It is important to understand the difference between conversion modes. Page Conversion renders the entire layout of each PDF page (including text, margins, and graphics) as a single JPG image. Image Extraction ignores text and page layouts, scanning the PDF's internal database to pull only the raw, embedded illustrations and photo files. Make sure to choose the correct setting for your project requirements to save time and file space.
