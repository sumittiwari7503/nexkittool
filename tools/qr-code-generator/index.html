What Is a QR Code?

A QR code (Quick Response code) is a two-dimensional barcode that can be scanned by a smartphone camera to instantly access information — a website URL, contact details, WiFi credentials, a text message, or any other data. QR codes became universally used after smartphone cameras integrated QR scanning natively.

Businesses use QR codes on menus, business cards, product packaging, and marketing materials. Individuals use them for sharing WiFi passwords, social profiles, and contact information.

Common QR Code Use Cases

  • Restaurant menus: Replace printed menus with QR codes that link to an online menu.
  • Business cards: Add a QR code to your business card that opens your LinkedIn profile or website.
  • WiFi sharing: Generate a QR code for your home or office WiFi — guests scan to connect instantly.
  • Product packaging: Link customers to product manuals, tutorials, or reorder pages.
  • Event tickets: Use QR codes as scannable entry tickets for events.
  • Social media: Create a QR code that links to your Instagram, YouTube, or TikTok profile.
  • Marketing campaigns: Track offline-to-online conversion by using QR codes in print ads.

QR Code Best Practices

  • Always test your QR code with multiple devices before printing or distributing.
  • Ensure adequate contrast between the QR code and background for reliable scanning.
  • For print materials, use SVG format for sharp, high-resolution output at any size.
  • Keep the landing page mobile-friendly — most QR scans happen on smartphones.
  • Include a brief call-to-action near the QR code: "Scan to visit our menu" or "Scan to connect to WiFi."
